GOMMA

A reusable base for any project. Dark panels on near-black, white pills, decoration reserved for the one promo or CTA surface in a view. Structure comes from fill steps and gaps, never from borders or shadows.

Separation without borders

The Spotify web player separates every surface with a one-step lighter fill and a black gap. GOMMA takes the same logic. A typical generated dark UI outlines every card, adds a shadow and puts a hairline between sections; none of those appear here.

The five rules

1

Lighter means closer

Each surface sits one step above its parent on the ramp. Never the same fill, never two steps.

2

The gap is the divider

Navbar, header, cards and panels float on bg-base with an 8px gap. The ground showing through is the rule.

3

Decoration marks the highlight

Tint, sheen and arcs appear only on a promo or CTA surface, one per view. KPIs, tables, forms and nav stay plain.

4

Interaction is a fill change

Hover moves the fill up one step. Buttons scale to 1.04. The one hard line is the 3px white focus ring.

5

An outline means optional

The 1px border-essential outline marks low-priority controls such as the language chip. It is never a surface edge.

Applying it

  • New surface: take the parent's token and go one step up. Stop there.
  • Two items on the same level: separate them with space-2 or more, not a line.
  • Tempted to add a border: raise the fill one step or widen the gap instead.
  • Tempted to decorate a card: ask whether it is the promo or CTA. If not, leave it a plain fill.

Roles

  • 3 is the brand surface. It is what promo and CTA surfaces already use (#014d78 and #015c59). The sheen and decorative lines sit on level 3 only.
  • 4 is hover and press. One step lighter, text goes to text-base. It carries the same rule as the neutral ramp: interaction is a fill change.
  • 5 is strongest. Selected state or a chip on level 3. White text only.
  • 0, 1, 2 are for a fully branded region such as onboarding or a marketing view: ground, panel, card. The standard app stays on the neutral ramp, so these are not used for tables, forms or KPIs.

Vivid surface (level 6)

One step above the brand level, for the occasional surface that must lift above everything else: #019df7 blue and #03c3bb teal. It carries the same sheen and the same decoration as level 3. Because it is much lighter, it is a different tier of emphasis and not a hover state.

Surface and ink pairs

A surface is never a fill with a global text color laid over it. It is a locked pair: the surface, the ink that sits on it, and a softer ink for secondary text, chosen and contrast-tested together. Everything drawn on the surface uses that ink: text, icons, strokes, the decorative lines and the CTA.

The rule: every surface is a pair. No component sets its own text or fill color. A new surface gets a pair first, then a component reads --surface, --ink and --ink-soft from it. This applies to the page ground, panels, cards, inputs, table rows, menus, buttons, badges and highlights alike.

How a pair behaves

  • Three values, one pair. --surface, --ink and --ink-soft. A component reads these and never a fixed text color, so the same card works on any pair.
  • One ink, no grey. Secondary text is --ink-soft, the ink pulled toward the surface in OKLab, so it keeps the hue. Hierarchy otherwise comes from size and weight.
  • Tonal or accent. Most pairs are tonal: the ink is the same hue family as the surface at a far lightness (navy on paper, deep teal on mist, white on blue). Accent pairs use a different, purposeful hue (amber, rose), reserved for status.
  • The CTA inverts the pair. The pill is filled with --ink and labelled with --surface, so it is always the strongest control on the surface. This is the default button.
  • Nested surfaces step up one pair. A raised fill inside a panel, a menu above it, a hover state on a row: each is the next neutral pair, with its own ink, not the parent's ink on a new fill.
  • Icons and lines use the ink. Strokes are currentColor. The decorative lines and arcs stay white on dark pairs; on inverse and accent pairs they are not used.
  • Tested at the worst point. Where a pair has the sheen, contrast is the worst case across the gradient, from its darkest to its lightest corner.
  • One exception: links. A text link uses --link #60a5fa on the neutral pairs. It is a semantic color, not a surface ink.

Tiers

  • Neutral (ground, panel, raised, press, strong): the default ramp. Most of the app.
  • Brand (blue, teal at level 3): the highlight surface. Sheen and decoration allowed.
  • Vivid (level 6): occasional, dark ink. Instead of brand, never with it.
  • Inverse (paper, paper blue, mist): light pairs on the dark UI for rare, high-emphasis moments such as a receipt, a printable statement or a share card. No sheen, no decoration.
  • Accent (amber and rose light; warning and danger dark): status only. The warning and danger pairs are what the status badges use.

Tint and texture

A highlight surface is a flat tint, a diagonal sheen from darker top-left to lighter bottom-right, and three hairline arcs anchored to the right edge. It is the only decorative element in the system, and it is reserved for a promo or a call to action. Everything else is a plain fill.

Data surfaces such as KPI cards use bg-elevated with no tint, sheen or arcs, so the highlight is the only thing that reads as emphasis.

Anatomy

  • Tint: #014d78 blue or #015c59 teal. Neutral #1c1c1e is the same as a plain panel, so it is not a highlight.
  • Sheen: linear-gradient(111.4deg, rgba(0,0,0,.2) 2%, rgba(102,102,102,.2) 95%) over the tint.
  • Arcs: inline SVG, not an image. Three 272 × 257 ellipses in one <symbol id="arcs">, 1px stroke at 28% white via --arc, clipped by the surface and anchored right.
  • Radius: 8px. No border, no shadow. Text stays text-base and text-subdued; the button on it is the white primary pill.
  • Where: a promo banner or a CTA card. Announcements sit at the top of the page content, directly under the navbar and above the page header. One per view, never in a table or form, never on a KPI. It is either a brand (level 3) or a vivid (level 6) surface.

Decorative lines

Dense bundles of hairlines that converge to a point and fan out, like a twisted ribbon. Monochrome white lines blended with overlay, so the surface keeps its own color. They replace the concentric arcs as the highlight motif; the arcs remain as the plainest fallback.

Glow and color drift

Inspired by the orb technique, applied to the decor and not as a shape, and rendered by a shader. One WebGL2 fragment shader draws the gradient and the ribbon lines together. The gradient is a soft mesh of color fields that drift slowly across the right of the surface under a gentle warp, trading two vivid fills back and forth. There is nothing radial or conic in it, so it never pinches into rays. The ribbons are the same Bezier bundles as the Sweep and Veil motifs, drawn per pixel, and their tint drifts with the gradient. The canvas is composited with screen over the surface, so the surface keeps its own color.

  • Slow and subtle: the color fields drift over roughly 30 to 70 seconds and the two colors trade places over about 28. The gradient peaks at about 40% strength. Under prefers-reduced-motion the shader draws one static frame and stops.
  • Color from two variables: the shader reads --decor-a and --decor-b from the canvas. A project points them at its brand's two vivid fills.
  • Exact ribbons: each bundle is a patch between two cubic edges. Per pixel the shader inverts the patch with Newton's method and draws constant-width iso-lines of the result, so the curves match the SVG motifs at any size. Lines are brighter toward the top edge, breathe with a travelling phase, and fade out toward the copy.
  • Copy stays clear: the gradient fades out toward the left edge and lives in the right 60 percent of the surface, so ink contrast is set by the left side. Keep text out of it.
  • Quiet by construction: 30 frames per second, a 2x pixel ratio cap, paused when offscreen or in a hidden tab. Without WebGL2 the component renders the CSS and SVG decor instead (css/decor-motion.css), so a surface is never left bare.
  • Same rules as the lines: one highlight surface per view, a brand or vivid pair, under the navbar. One shader canvas per view, never alongside the lines.
  • Use it: copy lib/decor-shader.ts and react/decor-shader.tsx into the project; for plain HTML, load docs/decor-shader.js and call createDecorShader(canvas, { motif }).

What keeps them quiet

  • Fade toward the text: every stroke runs through one gradient, transparent at the left edge, 10% at 30%, 50% at the right. The copy side stays clear.
  • Hairlines, not shapes: 0.8px strokes with opacity falling across each bundle, so the bundle has an edge and a shadow side without any fill.
  • Overlay blend: mix-blend-mode: overlay (token --decor-blend). White lines lighten the tint beneath them instead of sitting on top as grey, so each line is a lighter shade of the surface. Multiply was tested and rejected: white times the tint is the tint, so the lines disappear.
  • Monochrome: white only. The tint supplies the color, so the same motif works on blue, teal or any future tint. The surface is isolation: isolate so the blend never reaches the page behind it.
  • Built from two edges: each bundle is the blend between two cubic curves, one path per line. 30 to 60 short paths, under 10 KB a set.
  • Anchored right, clipped: preserveAspectRatio="xMaxYMid slice" fills the card height and pins the art to the right.
  • Motion is optional, slow and small: the lines may breathe (the shader does this; the CSS version is its fallback). Each rotates under 2 degrees about its bundle's origin over a 16 second cycle, phase-shifted per line so a soft wave moves across the fan, with the opacity easing between 80% and 100%. It stops under prefers-reduced-motion. No other chrome animates. One highlight surface per view; Glow is for a CTA only.

Start a project

The base is brand-agnostic. A project adds one brand, imports four CSS files and follows the pair rule.

  • 1. Generate your brand. python3 tools/brand.py "#2d4fb3" --name brand --out brands/brand.css. It writes the ramp (levels 0 to 5 plus vivid 6) and four pairs: brand, brand-vivid, brand-paper, brand-mist. Every ink and soft ink is searched until it passes 4.5:1, tested across the sheen. Add --vivid "<hex>" to set the vivid fill yourself; it warns, and suggests a lighter one, if no ink can pass 4.5:1 on it.
  • 2. Import the CSS. In order: css/tokens.css, css/pairs.css, your brands/brand.css, and for shadcn + Tailwind v4 css/shadcn-theme.css.
  • 3. Set the shell. <html class="dark"> and <body data-pair="ground">. Set --font-ui to your typeface and use weights 400 and 700.
  • 4. Use pairs, always. Panels and cards are data-pair="panel", inputs and zebra rows raised, menus press, status warning and danger. Highlights use react/tint-surface.tsx with a brand pair. Copy CLAUDE.template.md into the project as CLAUDE.md so the rule is enforced in every session.
  • 5. shadcn. shadcn/ holds retuned button, card, input, table, badge, dropdown-menu and avatar (base-ui style). They read the pairs; drop them over the generated ones.
  • 6. Decoration. react/decor-shader.tsx with lib/decor-shader.ts renders the gradient and ribbons as a shader; set --decor-a and --decor-b to your brand's vivid fills. decor/ and react/decor-sweep.tsx are the SVG versions and the fallback. One highlight surface per view, under the navbar.
